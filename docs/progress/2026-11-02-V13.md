# V13 — Matt, Spectrum, glazing and rear additions

## User instructions handled

- Continue from the Matt already present in the vehicle, with one articulated character and independent standing/driving and mask visibility controls.
- Reduce Spectrum's cylindrical shape using the supplied replica photographs.
- Move Matt toward a human anatomical interpretation rather than an articulated toy. The user explicitly authorized a realistic human interpretation of the supplied illustrated portrait on 2026-11-02, without likeness to any particular real person; authorization is recorded in `AGENTS.md`.
- Give automotive glazing slight tint, transparency and reflections.
- Hide the trunk interior with a parcel shelf and place a M.A.S.K. logo plate between the two reactors.

## Character and mask

The independent V3 mesh fingerprints were first checked against the vehicle: 270 mesh datablocks matched. The subsequent V4 art pass preserves the same Matt rig, head object and weighted flight suit while adapting anatomical topology. No alternate Matt is created for a pose or helmet state.

Generic head anatomy from Blender Human Base Meshes and Dan Ulrich's realistic hand asset were adapted to Matt. The CC0 archive, editable source, inspected thumbnails and provenance are preserved in `art_source/AnatomyBase/`. Human anatomy and unshown details remain interpretation, not photographic evidence of an official Matt costume or face. Gloves are continuous meshes with a driven driving-grip shape; hand roll changes in driving pose. Cloth shoulder volumes were reduced and knee patches follow the same deformed garment surface.

Spectrum has a domed head-shaped shell, raked brow, tapered lower face, triangular respirator, dark visor and joined double red neck seal. The supplied replica photographs and illustrated portrait were inspected directly. Intermediate renders exposed crown ridges, intersecting cheek overlays and gaps between collar rings; these were corrected and rendered again.

Controls on `MattTrakker_Rig`: `driving_pose=0` standing, `driving_pose=1` driving; `mask_on=False` face visible, `mask_on=True` Spectrum visible over the same retained head. French help is embedded in the standalone Matt file. Hair is hidden under the helmet.

## Glazing

The previous viewport was in SOLID mode with an opaque grey display color. This mode does not evaluate glass transparency. Large disk studio lights also produced white reflections that filled much of the glazing in actual renders. V13 uses physical Glass BSDF, IOR 1.50, roughness 0.025 and light green-grey tint/absorption; existing pane thickness is retained. Narrow rectangular studio lights preserve reflections while allowing cabin visibility. Tint strength is an artistic estimate, not a measured automotive specification.

## Rear additions

`THK_ParcelShelf` and its front lip occlude the trunk beneath the rear glass. Nine downward ray tests across the shelf find an opaque surface. `THK_RearPlate_MASK_Logo` sits on a fixed central mount between the two reactors. The supplied portrait's actual M.A.S.K. wordmark is sampled through editable UV mapping; the reference image is unchanged and packed in the source file. No registration number is invented.

The rear cover translates behind the plate. Its support passes below the cover and then behind it, instead of through its moving path. Dimensions and support construction are interpreted under the user's explicit request.

## Validation and limits

Final report: `art_source/Thunderhawk/validation_v13.json`. All 193 integer transform frames were checked for the tested surface pairs: helmet/body against selected doors, roof and glazing; plate against rear cover/reactors; shelf against selected moving parts. No tested surface intersections were detected. Both reactors remain retracted until the cover has fully risen and retract before it descends. The cover translates vertically; spoiler and two named door cannons remain separate.

Hands contact the wheel geometrically; footwear/pedal vertex distances remain approximately 0.5 mm, and sole/footwell distances approximately 1.45 mm. Seat cushioning intersects the flight suit; cloth/upholstery compression is not simulated. Surface-pair checks at integer frames are not a continuous-time or volumetric collision certification and do not cover every vehicle part pair.

Final renders and view metadata: `art_source/Thunderhawk/renders/v13/`. Face, profile, helmet, both standing states, both driving states, road glass, rear road/flight, plate and shelf views were inspected. Cabin views hide the windshield for inspection; the shelf cutaway hides rear glass. Other road/rear views preserve glazing. A Blender process restarted during an early review; saved sources survived and subsequent review queues completed. An intermediate patch-source lookup failed and was corrected before final renders.

The initial component exporter crashed Blender 5.2.3 inside `BKE_view_layer_copy_data` during `bpy.data.libraries.write(scene)`. The saved assembly and renders were intact. The crash dialog was inspected and Blender restarted; component export then succeeded through ordinary scene pruning and file saves, still executed by MCP. Use `export_assets_v13_safe.py`. The original exporter is retained as history but explicitly disabled to prevent re-running the known crash.

Independent files are reopened and verified in `standalone_validation_v13.json`. This is an improved anatomical art pass, not a certified photoreal or final game asset. Likeness, hairline/groom, cloth detail, skin texture, finger deformation and seat fit still need artistic refinement. Shipping retopology, UVs and Unreal integration are not completed in this pass.
